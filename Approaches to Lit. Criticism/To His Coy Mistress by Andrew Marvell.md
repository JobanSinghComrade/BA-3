# "To His Coy Mistress" in Simple English

**Who wrote it?** Andrew Marvell, an English poet, in the 1600s.

**What is it about?** A man talks to a woman he loves. She is "coy," which means shy. She says "no" to him again and again. He tries to convince her to love him now.

## Part 1: If we had more time (lines 1-20)

The man says: "If we had a lot of time, your shyness would be fine. We could walk slowly and talk all day."

He imagines a very long love story:

- You could be by the river Ganges in India. I could be by the river Humber in England.
- I would love you for many, many years. You could say "no" for thousands of years.
- I would spend a hundred years looking at your eyes. I would spend thirty thousand years on the rest of you.

He is joking. He is saying that she deserves all this time.

## Part 2: But we do not have time (lines 21-32)

Now the poem changes. He says: "But I always hear Time coming fast behind me, like a chariot with wings."

- Ahead of us is only a long, empty death.
- Your beauty will go away.
- When you are dead, no one will hear my love songs.
- Worms will eat your body. Your "honour" will become dust.
- The grave is quiet and private, but nobody hugs there.

This part is dark and a little scary. He wants her to see that life is short.

## Part 3: So let us love now! (lines 33-46)

The man gives his answer: "So, while you are young and beautiful, let us enjoy love now!"

- He compares them to hungry birds. They should eat time, and not let time eat them.
- They should put all their strength and sweetness together into one ball.
- They cannot stop the sun, but they can make it move faster. This means: "We cannot stop time, but we can enjoy it fully."

## Main ideas

1. **Time is short.** Life is not long, so do not wait.
2. **Carpe diem.** This is Latin for "seize the day." Enjoy today.
3. **Death comes for everyone.** Beauty and love do not last forever.

## The tone

The poem is funny at first, then serious, then passionate. The man is clever, but he is also trying to win an argument. Many readers ask: is he really in love, or does he only want to persuade her? What do you think?

# Hard Words in the Poem

## Words from Part 1

- **Coy**: shy. A coy person says "no" or waits before saying "yes."
- **Mistress**: here it means a woman a man loves. It does not mean a teacher.
- **Crime**: something bad that breaks the law. He says your shyness would not be a crime.
- **Ganges**: a big river in India.
- **Humber**: a river in England. Marvell lived near it.
- **Rubies**: red stones that cost a lot of money.
- **Complain**: to say you are sad or unhappy. He would be sad because she says "no."
- **The flood**: a big flood from the Bible, very long ago.
- **Conversion of the Jews**: a time far in the future. People believed it would come near the end of the world. He means "a very, very long time."
- **Vegetable love**: love that grows slowly, like a plant.
- **Adore**: to love very much.

## Words from Part 2

- **Chariot**: an old kind of cart pulled by horses.
- **Winged**: with wings. A chariot with wings is very fast.
- **Eternity**: time with no end.
- **Marble vault**: a stone room for dead people.
- **Echoing**: a sound that comes back again.
- **Worms**: small animals that live in the ground.
- **Virginity**: here, it means she has not had a lover yet.
- **Quaint honour**: an old-fashioned idea about being proper and good. He says it is a little silly.
- **Lust**: strong body love.
- **Dust and ashes**: what is left after a body is gone.

## Words from Part 3

- **Youthful hue**: the fresh color of young skin.
- **Transpires**: comes out. Her soul shows through her skin.
- **Sport**: to play and have fun.
- **Amorous**: full of love.
- **Birds of prey**: birds that hunt, like eagles.
- **Devour**: to eat very fast.
- **Languish**: to wait and feel weak and sad.
- **Slow-chapped power**: Time has slow jaws. It eats us a little at a time.
- **Strife**: a fight or hard work.
- **Iron gates of life**: the hard parts of life we must go through.

# Longer Summary

## The big idea

The poem is an argument in three steps: **If... But... Therefore...**

1. **If** we had forever, I could wait.
2. **But** we do not have forever.
3. **Therefore**, we should love each other now.

## Part 1 in more detail

The man starts in a playful way. He says her shyness is fine if they have enough time. He draws a picture of two lovers far away from each other. She is in India with rubies, and he is in England, sad. He loves her from before the flood, and she can say "no" until the end of the world.

Then he jokes about love that grows slowly. He says he would look at her eyes for one hundred years. He would spend two hundred years on each part of her body. He would need thirty thousand years for "the rest." He says she is worth all this time. The jokes are big and silly, and they make her smile.

## Part 2 in more detail

The mood changes with the word "But." The man speaks about death in a strong, scary way. He hears Time behind him, coming fast. In front of them there is only a huge, empty future.

He then talks about her body after death. Her beauty is gone. His songs are not heard. Worms are in her grave. He ends this part with a dark joke: the grave is a quiet, private place, but nobody hugs there.

## Part 3 in more detail

The last part starts with "Now." It is full of energy and short, strong words. He says she is young and her skin is bright, so they should not wait.

He uses pictures of hunting birds. The two lovers are not weak animals waiting for Time to eat them. They are birds, and they should eat Time first. Then he asks them to put all their strength and sweetness into one ball and push it through the gates of life.

The last two lines are the most famous. They cannot stop the sun, but they can make it run. We cannot make time slow, but we can fill it with life.

## Style notes

- **Big numbers** in Part 1 are a joke. They show he is not serious, at first.
- **Short, hard sounds** in Part 2 make death feel cold.
- **Quick, hot words** in Part 3 make the reader feel the hurry.

## Two ways to read the poem

1. **A love poem:** a man really loves a woman and does not want to waste time.
2. **A clever trick:** a man uses fear of death to get what he wants. Some readers think his argument is good, and some think it is not fair.

# **Historical:**

Many critics have examined literary work through a historical lens. **_History of English Literature_** is one of the many landmark works by the French critic and historian Hippolyte Taine. First published in French in the 1860s, it became one of the most influential works of literary criticism in the 19th century. It centers around a historical approach to the framework of literary criticism.

## Main Idea

Taine argued that literature is **not created in isolation**. Instead, it is shaped by three major forces:
#### 1. Race 

According to Hippolyte Taine, **Race** refers to the inherited cultural, historical, and psychological characteristics shared by a people over generations. It does **not** mean biological race in the modern sense but the collective traditions, customs, beliefs, language, and temperament that shape a nation's identity. These inherited qualities influence a writer's imagination, values, themes, and style, making literature a reflection of the enduring character and cultural heritage of a society.

#### 2. Milieu 

**Milieu** refers to the social, political, economic, geographical, and cultural environment in which an author lives and writes. Taine believed that a writer's surroundings significantly shape literary works by influencing experiences, perspectives, and concerns. Factors such as climate, class structure, education, religion, government, and social customs affect both the content and style of literature. Thus, literary works mirror the conditions and realities of the society from which they emerge.

#### 3. Moment 

**Moment** refers to the historical period and the prevailing intellectual, political, and cultural movements that influence an author. Taine argued that writers are shaped by the spirit of their age, including major events, philosophical ideas, scientific discoveries, and social transformations. Every era possesses a distinct outlook that affects literary themes, forms, and values. Consequently, literature reflects not only individual creativity but also the unique historical consciousness of the time in which it is produced.

This is often summarized as Taine's **"Race, Milieu, and Moment"** theory.

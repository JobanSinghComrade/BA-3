"Trail of the Green Blazer" is a short story by R. K. Narayan. It is about a pickpocket called Raju.

The story takes place in a busy, noisy market. Raju wears only a cloth around his waist and a big turban, so the police will think he is a village man. He is a lazy person, but he is good at one thing: he watches people and takes things from their pockets. He does not like pens and watches because they are hard to sell. He likes full purses best. He takes the money and throws the purse away.

Raju has a wife and children, and he buys them sweets, books and small gifts. His wife believes he earns "commission" money. She does not ask more questions, and Raju hides extra cash under a roof tile.

One day, Raju sees a man in a green blazer and follows him, always three steps behind. The man argues about the price of a coconut. Then he argues again about a balloon. He says it is for a boy with no mother. Raju thinks the man is mean and has a rough voice.

In the crowd, Raju steals the man's purse. He goes behind an old well and finds thirty rupees and some small coins. He is happy. The money will last until the end of the month, and he can take his family to the cinema.

Before he throws the purse into the well, Raju finds the balloon inside. He feels very sad. He imagines the little boy waiting at the door and the angry father with an empty purse. Raju thinks of his own young son. He decides the child must get his balloon. He cannot give back only the balloon, so he puts it in the empty purse. His plan is to put the purse back in the man's pocket.

This is a big mistake. The man catches Raju's arm and shouts "Pickpocket!" He hits Raju hard, and other people hit him too. Raju says he was only trying to put the purse back, but the crowd laughs at him.

The same thing happens in court. Everybody laughs, and the story becomes a joke among the police. Raju's wife visits him in jail and cries because of the shame. He stays there for eighteen months.

When Raju comes out, he makes a new decision. If he steals again, he will make sure he never has to put anything back. He believes God made his fingers only to take things, not to return them.

The story is funny, but it also shows that Raju has a kind heart, even though he is a thief.

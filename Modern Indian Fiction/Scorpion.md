Inderjit Singh Kataria, a lawyer from a low-caste weaving family (Ramdasia, originally "Jularh"), has left his mentor Chopra's annexe and built his own house in Model Town. He and Chopra, who is a Mazhabi, both took upper-caste surnames to leave their origins behind. Kataria's wife Dalbir complains about Chopra's wife, and the two couples trade caste slurs in private. In public they stay polite, calling each other "Sahib." Kataria has nearly forgotten his past: his father and grandfather's weaving, his scholarship days, and the humiliation of collecting aid as a "scheduled caste" child.

While the roof of his new house is being built, he rides to Arya Samaj Chowk to hire ten more labourers. The union board listing wages there unsettles him. He feels a brief surge of kinship with the workers, then covers it with haughtiness. He also hires a fair, handsome Punjabi boy who doesn't look like a daily wager.

On the ride, the boy shouts to a driver about collecting his pawned turban and spits a caste slur at him. He says he's from Kot Fateh, the village of Kataria's mother and her brother, Gulu Gajjan. At the site the boy proves clumsy and slow. He takes a tablet at the tap, seems drugged, and stares at Mrs Kataria. When asked, he gives his name as Hardam and says he is from the Tiwana (landowning Jat) family. Kataria realizes he is the grandson of the landlords who once humiliated his family. He feels a petty triumph at having this boy labour for him.

Hardam asks if Kataria has relatives in Kot Fateh, perhaps Gulu chamiyar. Kataria denies it and is immediately overwhelmed by guilt, shame and the weight of all the lies he has told to escape his caste. At lunchtime he plans to decide how to treat the boy, perhaps with kindness, perhaps with the contempt the landlords once showed. Then Hardam claims a scorpion has stung his thumb and asks for thirty rupees for a doctor. There is no sting mark, and Kataria sees he is shamming. The boy's look says he knows exactly who Kataria is. Kataria feels as if a scorpion has stung him in the heart.

The story is about how caste identity, reinvented and denied, still stings. Kataria's upward mobility never frees him from the wound. The "scorpion" of the title is the boy's knowing, quiet exposure of him, not a literal sting.

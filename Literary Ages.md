| Age              | Timeline     | Brief explanation                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Dark Ages        | 449-1066     | Characterized by invasions, political fragmentation, and limited surviving literature; Anglo-Saxon poetry, especially Beowulf, represents its strongest literary achievement.                            |
| Chaucerian Age   | 1340-1400    | Dominated by Geoffrey Chaucer, this age marks Middle English literary development, blending medieval traditions with realism, satire, and vernacular expression.                                         |
| Elizabethan Age  | 1558-1603    | Named after Elizabeth I, this celebrated Renaissance period produced Shakespeare, Marlowe, Spenser, Sidney, and remarkable developments in drama and poetry.                                             |
| Jacobean Age     | 1603-1625    | Corresponding to James I's reign, literature became darker and more complex, featuring Shakespeare's later plays, Donne, Jonson, and metaphysical poetry.                                                |
| Caroline Age     | 1625-1649    | Under Charles I, courtly Cavalier poetry flourished alongside metaphysical writing, emphasizing elegance, loyalty, love, wit, and religious reflection.                                                  |
| Puritan Age      | 1649-1660    | Following the monarchy's overthrow, Puritan influence dominated culture; Milton became its greatest literary figure through religious, political, and epic writings.                                     |
| Restoration Age  | 1660-1700    | Charles II's restoration encouraged secularism, wit, satire, and sophisticated comedy, with Dryden becoming the period's leading poet, critic, and dramatist.                                            |
| Augustan Age     | 1700-1745    | Influenced by classical ideals, this age valued reason, order, satire, and balance, producing Pope, Swift, Addison, Steele, and Johnson.                                                                 |
| Pre-Romantic Age | 1745-1798    | Literature increasingly emphasized emotion, nature, imagination, melancholy, and individual experience, preparing the way for Romanticism through transitional poets and writers.                        |
| Romantic Age     | 1798-1832    | Beginning conventionally with Lyrical Ballads, Romanticism celebrated imagination, nature, emotion, individualism, freedom, and ordinary life through revolutionary poetic expression.                   |
| Victorian Age    | 1837-1901    | Under Queen Victoria, literature confronted industrialization, social inequality, morality, science, faith, and progress through novels, poetry, essays, and criticism.                                  |
| Modern Age       | 1901-1945    | Marked by experimentation and uncertainty, Modernist writers responded to war, fragmentation, urbanization, psychology, and collapsing traditional values through innovative forms.                      |
| Post-Modern Age  | 1945-present | Postmodern literature questions absolute truths, embraces fragmentation, intertextuality, irony, popular culture, multiple perspectives, and experimentation across increasingly diverse literary forms. |
